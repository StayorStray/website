Hidden Gems in Kansas City

Ten Kansas City places most visitors drive past, from an underground business park and a steamboat full of 1856 cargo to the riverside spot where Lewis and Clark camped, each with real photos, how to get there and one practical tip.

01 · Fairfax / Kaw Point, Kansas City, KS

Kaw Point Riverfront Park

Riverside park where the Kansas (Kaw) River meets the Missouri, with skyline views of downtown Kansas City, Missouri.

Kaw Point Park is a 10-acre wooded park in Kansas City, Kansas, on the Kansas–Missouri river confluence. The Lewis and Clark Expedition camped here June 26–28, 1804; the park is a Lewis and Clark National Historic Trail site with an amphitheater, a memorial to the area's Native tribes, and views across to downtown Kansas City, Missouri.

Tip: The confluence in 1804 was likely about a quarter mile north of today's point, because the rivers have moved. Riverbanks can be muddy after rain.

02 · Underground complex near Worlds of Fun, Kansas City, MO

SubTropolis

A vast underground business complex in a former limestone mine, entered through tunnels cut into the bluffs above the Missouri River in Kansas City.

SubTropolis is a former limestone mine developed by Hunt Midwest beginning in 1964 into an underground business park. It holds warehouses, offices and other businesses, and stays at roughly 65–70°F (18–21°C) year-round. It is mainly a working business park, not a tourist attraction.

Tip: Public access is limited: it is a private business complex, and tours happen only through organized events or groups. Do not expect regular public tours, restaurants or attractions. Cell signal can be weak underground.

03 · River Market, Kansas City, MO

Arabia Steamboat Museum

Museum of the steamboat Arabia, which sank in the Missouri River in 1856 and was dug out of a Kansas cornfield in 1988.

The Arabia sank in the Missouri River in 1856. Because the river later changed course, the wreck ended up under farmland, and a family team excavated it in 1988–89. The museum in the River Market shows a full-size reconstruction of the hull and paddlewheel, along with the pre–Civil War cargo recovered from the mud.

Tip: Check ticket prices and hours on the official site (1856.com). The River Market area has many places to eat nearby.

04 · Crown Center / Penn Valley Park, Kansas City, MO

National WWI Museum and Memorial (Liberty Memorial tower)

America's national World War I museum, beneath the Liberty Memorial tower.

The Liberty Memorial was dedicated in 1926. Congress later designated the National WWI Museum and Memorial as America's official World War I museum. The museum below the tower tells the story of the Great War, and the memorial grounds look out over downtown Kansas City.

Tip: Check the official site (theworldwar.org) for tickets, tower access and hours. The grounds and the view of the tower from the lawn are outdoors.

05 · Westside, Kansas City, MO

Boulevard Brewing Company

Kansas City's best-known craft brewery, with tours and a beer hall in the Westside.

Boulevard Brewing Company still operates its brewery campus on Southwest Boulevard. Visitors can join public brewery tours, which end with samples in the tasting room, or stop by the Beer Hall and gift shop without taking a tour.

Tip: Tours and tastings are for adults of legal drinking age; the Beer Hall welcomes all ages. Some specialty tours involve stairs. Check prices on boulevard.com.

06 · West Bottoms, Kansas City, MO

West Bottoms

A former stockyards district of brick warehouses, now known for its monthly First Friday weekend antique and vintage markets.

The West Bottoms is the river-bottom district below the bluffs west of downtown, once home to the Kansas City stockyards. Today its large brick warehouses hold antique, vintage and salvage shops. Many of them open mainly on First Friday weekends, the first Friday through Sunday of each month. A standout is the Hobbs Building at 1427 W. 9th St., a red-brick former industrial building from the early 1900s that was bought and saved in 1997 and now houses studios and The Hobbs event space.

Tip: Many shops open only on market weekends, so a weekday visit may find things closed. Expect crowds and limited parking on market weekends.

07 · Weston, MO (~30 mi NW of Kansas City)

Weston Historic District (day trip, Weston, MO)

A historic river town about 30 miles northwest of Kansas City, with the 1845 St. George Hotel on its main street.

Weston's historic district preserves a 19th-century Missouri River town. The St. George Hotel, which traces its history to 1845, still runs as a hotel today. It is a contributing building on Main Street, surrounded by shops and wineries.

Tip: The St. George Hotel is operating. One booking listing mentions guestroom renovations during 2026, so check with the hotel directly. Note that this is a day trip outside Kansas City.

08 · Pendleton Heights / Northeast, Kansas City, MO

Kansas City Museum (Corinthian Hall)

A city history museum in Corinthian Hall, the restored R.A. Long mansion in Kansas City's Historic Northeast.

The Museum of Kansas City occupies Corinthian Hall, the former mansion of lumber businessman R.A. Long, at 3218 Gladstone Blvd. The city owns the property. After a restoration that began in 2014, Corinthian Hall reopened in 2021 with exhibits on Kansas City's history. Downstairs is Elixir, a soda fountain.

Tip: Visit KC lists general admission as free; check the official site for current details. Work on the outbuildings is ongoing.

09 · Independence, MO (Kansas City metro)

Vaile Mansion

An 1881 Second Empire mansion in Independence, Missouri, in the Kansas City area.

The Vaile Mansion at 1500 N. Liberty St in Independence, Missouri, is an 1881 Victorian home in the Second Empire style. It opens for guided tours during its spring–fall season and again for holiday tours.

Tip: It is seasonal and closed outside the tour seasons. Tickets are sold at the door. Groups of 15 or more should email ahead. Check prices on vailemansion.org.

10 · Waldo / State Line Rd, Kansas City, MO

Alexander Majors House

An 1856 frontier home on the Missouri–Kansas state line, built by the freighter behind Russell, Majors & Waddell.

Alexander Majors built this house in 1856. It was his family home and the headquarters of his freighting firm, Russell, Majors & Waddell, which later backed the Pony Express. It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and is one of Kansas City's few surviving pre–Civil War buildings. It has been open to the public as a museum since 1984.

Tip: Visits are mostly by appointment outside the May–October weekend schedule. The NPS notes the house was most likely built with the labor of enslaved people. Check prices on wornallmajors.org.
